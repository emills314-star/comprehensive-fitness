## Zero-based redesign M1 decision boundary — IMPLEMENTED LOCALLY

The named exercise variant, not the normal recommendation engine, now owns the ordinary set count/roles, rep range, target-RPE band, rest default, and increment. Historical engine snapshots remain immutable compatibility material, but their exact set/load/rep actions do not rewrite stable routine targets and are not editable-field prefill sources. M4 removes the normal current snapshot/plan-preview producers. **IMPLEMENTED (M5):** a legacy exact rep target remains exact; one reps edit synchronizes that set's lower and upper rep bounds to the same value rather than widening or silently replacing the target range.

Normal prefill is factual and ordered:

1. the corresponding prior working set from the most recent comparable session for the exact variant and role/index;
2. an explicit routine load/repetition/RPE default for that role;
3. blank.

Top, back-off, straight, drop, and warm-up records are not substituted for one another. Prior same-role RPE is an actual-input prefill and remains distinct from the stable target-RPE band. Internal edit helpers cannot change the M1 prefill or create a visible recommendation. The canonical exception resolver is implemented and cannot silently widen or replace the stable rep range.

Global equipment preference and app-level exercise exclusions are no longer inputs for normal workout projection, substitution, or mutation authorization. Old equipment fields, excluded-ID settings, and equipment-only blocked snapshots are compatibility/history inputs and cannot prevent an exercise from being logged. Pain and illness remain advisory exception inputs; canonical performance identity remains a history-integrity boundary rather than a logging gate.

### Superseded guided-planning record — HISTORICAL

The introductory guide and viability evaluator consume the same exported `PLANNING_RULES` object. Normal sessions should remain at or below 18 working sets; more than two direct exercises for one muscle in one day is a strong warning; normal/priority muscles default to two meaningful weekly exposures, maintenance to one, and specialization to at least two. Direct sets, exercise-specific fractional contribution, weighted stimulus, and isometric exposure remain separate.

The engine's `aggregateMuscleResearchDefaults` adapter is the authoritative guided-builder research target boundary. It reads `minimum_effective_weekly_sets` and `typical_effective_weekly_sets_low/high`, then the view applies the existing maintenance or specialization presentation adjustment. The application retains a dormant `{ min: 6, target: 10, max: 14 }` compatibility fallback when no adapter range exists. That fallback is not described as a research-derived range: it carries `evidenceFallback: true`, renders as categorical `research_default` / UI level 1 with a dashed outline, and is covered as a provisional missing-data path.

Target-muscle candidate eligibility requires canonical `direct_load` or positive-credit `meaningful_fractional_load` (legacy `primary`/`secondary` remain schema-compatible). Isometric stabilizing, incidental, unknown, and zero-credit relationships are excluded even when a private personal-derived row claims secondary involvement. `scores.targetMuscleEffectiveness` combines canonical specificity with target-relevant progression, recovery, research support, and fatigue efficiency; the UI shows that 0–100 score beside the engine's `scores.weightingReason`. Candidate evidence certainty is separate: categorical `candidate.personalDataConfidence` drives the four-level meter, while adjacent `candidate.scores.personalDataConfidence` is a numeric 0–100 evidence-quality value. The latter must not be coerced into `data-conf`.

Empty configured days are technical blockers. Unusual but usable volume or frequency choices are warnings the user may explicitly accept. Cross-day reuse of the same canonical exercise is allowed and creates no finding. Any assignment, move, or set edit makes the prior viability result stale.

Viability begins at 100 and deducts for unresolved blockers, strong warnings, and advisories. Grades are Excellent (90+), Good (80+), Workable (70+), and Needs Revision. Presentation groups the three engine severities as Blocking, Warnings, and Advisory. The engine's `readyToGenerate` is the sole creation gate; only unresolved technical blockers prevent linked-template creation.

Candidate gap preview is a presentation query over the existing pure planning functions, not a new score or approximation. The view applies the candidate to a temporary draft with `addExercise`, recomputes `volumeLedger`, reads the matching `muscleTargetStatuses` result, then discards the temporary draft. The real draft is not mutated until the user confirms the assignment.

The current Create comparison does not claim completed-block execution. No per-muscle actual-volume aggregate exists on the mesocycle completion record; the completion outcome currently retains coarse metadata such as `completedFromApp: true`. The UI therefore compares a positive comparable previous plan with the current plan, or shows the current plan alone. Deriving mesocycle execution volume is **PLANNED** engine/data-contract work in `docs/ROADMAP.md`.

Guided muscle status is computed once from total effective sets (`direct + credited fractional`), exposure frequency, and distribution. A muscle is successful only when every applicable rule passes. Priority is blocking/invalid, fatigue or recovery risk, above target, missing frequency, below target, poor distribution, then fully valid. A fractional-only total may satisfy volume; it does not satisfy frequency unless qualifying work occurs across the required exposures. Remaining volume is `max(0, minimum target - total effective sets)` and retains half-set precision when applicable. Build, Needs Attention, Completed, Viability, Review, and Create consume the same status object.

The engine selects exercises, creates versioned prescriptions and mesocycles, adjusts a workout for current readiness, explains decisions, and preserves the exact recommendation used. It is deterministic for the same normalized inputs; research translation and some operational thresholds are heuristic.

Implemented precedence is tested as:

1. Credible high-confidence personal evidence.
2. Moderate personal evidence blended with research.
3. Sparse/low-confidence personal evidence with greater research weight.
4. Research defaults when meaningful personal evidence is absent.

Fewer than three comparable exposures remain research-led (`DEFAULT_POLICY.minimumComparableExposures`, `scripts/test-prescription-engine.js`). Confidence considers exposure count/span, variation consistency, RPE, recovery, nutrition completeness, data completeness, and confounding (`derivePersonalEvidenceMetrics`, `calculateEvidenceWeight`). Exact IDs/aliases are preferred; unresolved variants remain separate.

### Prescription confidence presentation mapping — SUPERSEDED HISTORICAL RECORD (M4)

`calculateEvidenceWeight` already returns one categorical string in `confidence`; this is the confidence contract used by the Evidence & Change prescription UI. It is exactly four categories, not a numeric level supplied by the view:

| Engine `confidence` | Existing engine condition | UI `data-conf` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `research_default` | Fewer than `minimumComparableExposures` (default 3); `personalEvidenceWeight` is exactly 0 and research remains controlling | `1` |
| `low` | At least the minimum exposure count but below the moderate/high gates | `2` |
| `moderate` | `personalEvidenceWeight >= 0.4` and at least `moderateComparableExposures` (default 5) | `3` |
| `high` | `personalEvidenceWeight >= 0.68` and at least `highComparableExposures` (default 8) | `4` |

The numeric UI level is presentation-only and is not persisted or returned by the engine. The categorical string is retained on base/final prescriptions, recommendation snapshots, and surface projections. `personalEvidenceWeight` and `researchEvidenceWeight` remain separate 0–1 blend weights, while `personalDataConfidence` remains a separate 0–100 evidence-quality score. None is substituted for `confidence`.

Role-level progression may separately use `setPrescription.confidence: "conditional"` to describe whether a future increment is ready. That is not a fifth evidence-confidence category and is not mapped to `data-conf`. The claim UI reads the stored `evidenceConfidence`/snapshot confidence category instead. Missing or legacy non-contract confidence renders as unavailable rather than being coerced into the four levels.

Private exercise identity reconciliation examines all three executable personal sources: exercise scores, exercise prescriptions, and exercise-muscle scores. A newly selected evidence file or backup is rejected atomically when any reconciled identity is invalid; no runtime engine, IndexedDB record, revision, completed-analysis cache, prescription cache, or muscle-assignment cache is changed. A valid, collision-free personal ID remains executable regardless of whether it uses a newer `custom_`/`user_` naming convention. An invalid identity already present in persisted legacy data is preserved for audit and marked invalid by the reconciler, but identity, prescription, muscle assignment, history grouping, and volume consumers fail closed for that exercise. A successful evidence replacement invalidates every dependent cache before new results are consumed.

Full-backup import validates every active/template executable snapshot against the exported schema/checksum serializer and a prepared candidate engine before persistence. Canonical identity, the recorded exact direct target, and each exercise/template host binding must agree; validation does not create a fresh prescription. A stale checksum, unknown schema, collision, unrelated target, or host mismatch rejects the entire import with zero writes, engine/evidence replacement, runtime/revision change, or cache invalidation. A valid hard-safety rejection remains importable because it is explicitly non-executable. Submitted historical snapshots are excluded from this executable revalidation and remain byte-immutable.

### Canonical future-generation target boundary — ENGINE + FRONTEND IMPLEMENTED

Prescription engine `3.4.1` retains deterministic instance adapters and the snapshot/prescription `1.4.0`/`2.4.0` pair while extending the append-only override contract with role-specific top-set/back-off dose changes. `resolveExerciseIdentity(value)` resolves a canonical research ID, canonical exercise name, or any exported exercise alias under the engine's case/whitespace/separator normalization. Exact Strong labels are aliases only when the catalog declares their one canonical owner; the 84-alias registry resolves all 23 explicitly research-mapped private Strong labels without token-order or equipment-stripping heuristics. The frontend keeps the alias or trusted-personal performance identity separate from the canonical research identity, preventing light/heavy, attachment, and equipment variations from sharing prior-load history. A valid personal crosswalk resolves to its canonical research exercise. Conflicting public aliases return `ambiguous_public_exercise_identity`; an explicit personal identity that collides with a different public identity returns `personal_public_identity_collision`; invalid explicit crosswalks retain `invalid_reconciled_identity`; and an unmapped custom or unknown value returns `unknown_exercise_identity`. The frontend propagates any non-unknown engine reason without maintaining a fragile allowlist, so later collision classes fail closed. Broad display/reporting regions such as Back, Shoulders, Core, and Glutes are not exercise aliases and cannot become executable exercise identities.

`resolveDefaultPrescriptionTarget(value)` derives a future-generation default only when the resolved exercise has exactly one relationship with `relationship_type=direct_load`, `loading_role=dynamic|mixed`, and positive fractional-set credit. The result retains the exact canonical `mg_*` ID and returns the relationship's taxonomy version as transient resolver provenance. Multiple qualifying direct rows fail closed as `ambiguous_dynamic_direct_target`; zero qualifying rows, including zero-credit and isometric-only rows, fail closed as `no_dynamic_direct_target`. In taxonomy 2.1.0 this resolves 59 of 62 catalog exercises; Farmer's Carry, Pallof Press, and Side Plank remain intentionally ineligible for an automatic dynamic hypertrophy target.

This adapter does not broaden or replace explicit caller targets. `prescribeExercise` continues to accept a valid positive-credit fractional relationship and reject an unrelated target. The app adopts the adapter for future automatic generation: it resolves an identity profile, derives the exact default target, and only then calls the engine. `performanceExerciseId` selects exact prior-session evidence and therefore keeps named/equipment variations separate. Optional `researchExerciseId` selects taxonomy, muscle-family projection, and science defaults. A reconciled Double Pulley Lat Pulldown can therefore use its own prior loads while inheriting the public lat-pulldown research relationships; Chest Assisted Row can use its valid personal evidence without an artificial prefix. No name-based research target is fabricated for an unresolved or colliding exercise. Adapter failures remain typed zero-set/zero-rep/zero-load rejections and never reach `prescribeExercise`. At the separate workout-target layer, `unknown_exercise_identity`, `invalid_muscle_identity`, and `no_dynamic_direct_target` may become `history_fallback` only when exact submitted Strong history, matching resistance, and valid saved set structure exist. That fallback is loggable but carries no invented research identity or research-derived progression. Existing broad display/reporting projection remains a separate presentation concern.

Submitted historical snapshots are immutable. The frontend returns a snapshot attached to a submitted session before current identity or default-target resolution, with no migration, backfill, checksum refresh, or taxonomy-field injection. Older broad-target history therefore remains byte/reference-equivalent and readable. A stored active/template snapshot is different because it can execute: the frontend reuses it only after schema/checksum, canonical or declared-custom identity, target, and host validation. Failure returns a deterministic non-executable `invalid_stored_recommendation_snapshot` with zero sets/reps/load, performs no fresh prescribe call, and does not cache the rejected object. New engine 3.4.1 output uses snapshot/prescription `1.4.0`/`2.4.0`; repository-known earlier pairs remain read-only compatible.

## Readiness and recovery — SUPERSEDED ANALYTICAL RECORD (M4)

Engine-level `evaluateReadiness`/`readinessAdjustmentFor` is the canonical scoring path. `recoveryRecommendationForSession` adapts its result into UI labels; it does not calculate a second readiness score. If the engine is unavailable, the app holds the base plan rather than inventing a score. Illness/pain remains an immediate rest/modify guardrail.

Verified engine rules:

- Sleep is adverse below 85% of baseline or below 6 hours; below 70% of baseline is more severe.
- Soreness or fatigue at least 7 is adverse; at least 9 is more severe.
- Subjective readiness at most 4 is adverse; at most 2 is more severe when supplied.
- HRV below 90% of baseline or resting heart rate above 108% of baseline contributes a physiologic domain; more extreme deviation increases severity.
- Recent regression contributes a performance domain.
- Nutrition/protein inadequacy or low energy availability is one independent domain.
- A single isolated marker is monitored and does not rewrite today’s prescription.
- Nutrition alone cannot trigger a deload; an independent signal is required.
- Adverse multi-domain/persistent readiness can reduce working sets, load, and target RPE for one day; it does not rewrite the base prescription or mesocycle.

These thresholds are in `prescription-engine.js:evaluateReadiness` and tested in `scripts/test-prescription-engine.js`. They are operational heuristics, not universal medical cutoffs.

Positive readiness means “follow the plan and take already-planned progressions if warm-ups agree,” not “exceed the plan” (`recoverySessionDetail`, `recoveryRecommendationForSession`). The implemented concise labels include “Go as planned” and adjustment/rest variants. **NEEDS REVIEW:** exact copy differs across app- and engine-level paths.

Missing markers do not count as zeros. Sparse input reduces the number of independent domains available and normally preserves the base plan. Conflicting markers are retained as evidence; multiple independent adverse domains outweigh a lone positive marker. Confidence is expressed in snapshots/evidence, but there is no probabilistic readiness confidence interval.

The workout-start view compares entered readiness values with the persisted, user-configured `data.settings.readinessBaseline` after `cleanReadinessBaseline` normalization. That object contains sleep hours, sleep quality, HRV, resting heart rate, soreness, and the readiness band. It is not computed from workout history, and the view does not derive or update it on the fly. Missing entered metrics remain absent; missing stored HRV or resting-heart-rate values suppress the comparison delta. These comparisons are presentation only. Adjustment authority remains `evaluateReadiness`/`readinessAdjustmentFor`, and the review consumes the existing per-exercise `{ original, adjusted, changed, reason, triggers }` projection without creating a second rule or reason.

## Prescription, progression, and overload

`createExercisePrescriptionSnapshot` combines personal/research defaults, selected set structure, volume, progression, staleness, deload, readiness, and explanation. `createWorkoutPrescription` groups immutable exercise snapshots.

The app maps only recognized native `Error` constraint failures with exact supported messages to executable hard-constraint explanations. Unexpected exceptions, including lookalike `TypeError` messages, become a non-executable `engine_failure`; they cannot revive legacy Dashboard advice or be misreported as an ordinary equipment/exclusion condition.

Implemented progression considers exercise/resistance type, rep-range position, completion, RPE/RIR, prior comparable exposure, increment size, technique/pain flags, and trends. Outcomes include normal, progress, hold, reduced volume, light session, scoped/full deload, substitute, and rotate. Exact next actions can add reps, add/reduce load or assistance, hold, reduce volume, or change exercise. Isolation/bodyweight cases prefer rep progression when load jumps are disproportionate (`index.html:progressionProfileForExercise` and recommendation helpers; `determineProgressionDecision`).

The goal-aware progression contract is versioned through `training-profile/1.1.0`, `progression-confirmation/1.0.0`, and the current prescription/snapshot pair. Strength, hypertrophy, muscular-endurance, and general-fitness profiles alter rep/rest policy without bypassing the shared safety and evidence gates. Upward progression requires consecutive comparable exposures with completed prescribed work, valid technique, target effort, pain-free execution, and comparable performance; stable low-fatigue isolation work is the documented one-exposure exception. The final prescription's `progressionAction`, `progressionRule`, confirmation record, scientific provenance, prescribed load, and role rows are the auditable decision trace. The same rows prepopulate the active workout and drive its summary and rationale. The 2026 ACSM overview (`stu_0043`/`con_0028`) supports progressive resistance training and outcome-specific variation but is not treated as a universal threshold algorithm.

For comparable straight sets, load progression requires the first set to reach the top of the prescribed range within the RPE ceiling and every later set to remain within the configured acceptable rep-loss limit. Otherwise the next action is rep-first: keep load constant and add a repetition to the first eligible set below the retention threshold. Once the lead set and retained sets qualify, increase every straight set by the smallest supported equipment increment. A `progress` label is invalid unless load, reps, sets, assistance, execution target, or another explicit performance variable changes.

Active set cards distinguish evidence confidence from progression readiness. The recommendation snapshot's `finalPrescription` is authoritative for role structure, the history-derived top-load anchor, role ranges, RPE, and set count. Workout startup converts that final object—not `basePrescription`—into executable role rows. Each row then resolves its exact rep target from the newest comparable role/index: hold repeats the bounded prior reps, rep progression adds one, a load increase restarts at that role's lower bound, and a return baseline steps down one rep while staying inside the role range. No comparable role history falls back to the saved prescription target. Top-set load comes directly from `finalPrescription`; back-off load is the declared reduction from that same top anchor. The persisted generated rows remain the authoritative executable targets. The exercise claim projects those same rows as the human-readable prescription, including exact load, repetition target, and role composition, while the row inputs expose the values for editing rather than recomputing them. Role evidence headings retain the exact live set count per role; their facts supply prior performance, next increment, and the progression gate without duplicating the exercise-level confidence or creating another target claim. `basePrescription` exists only to explain readiness changes.

Presentation deltas do not create a second decision engine. Raw readiness differences are neutral facts until the readiness result supplies a judgment. When a delta is a judgment, the shared grammar is: `.on` for inside/success, `.under` for below target, `.over` only for desirable target exceedance, `.out` for exceeding an undesirable ceiling, and `.none` for no judgment. Guided muscle deltas derive from the same `overallStatus` as the adjacent pill, so the two cannot contradict. These mappings change presentation only; they do not alter scoring, readiness, volume, or progression outputs.

Snapshot `1.4.0` / prescription `2.4.0` add `standardGuideline/1.0.0`, captured after goal/personal policy but before prescribed-load, deload, readiness, or manual-override changes. The standard remains an immutable comparison, renders as a read-only program benchmark, and is never fabricated for an unmapped or unprofiled custom exercise. Separately, every active exercise exposes user-owned execution defaults even when no recommendation snapshot, research record, or prior target exists. Shared values cover working-set count, rep range, ordered target RPE range, working rest, and warm-up rest. The optional individual-set plan assigns Standard, Top, Back-off, or Drop type and an exact rep range, ordered target RPE range, and rest target to each set. Applying either shape appends an `exercise_default_targets` audit entry, changes unfinished rows only, and preserves completed work, actual results, recommendation history, and the immutable standard. Explicit template saving is opt-in and stores exact set targets through template `setTypes`/`warmups`; later workout construction and historical target resolution preserve their identity. Actual repetitions and logged RPE remain freely editable outcome evidence rather than rewriting the saved targets or invalidating the session.

**REMOVED IN M7:** the achievement-badge system, volume record, plan-complete reward, target-precision reward, and recovery/adherence badge no longer have a renderer, current derivation path, style, asset, or test contract. Completion may show at most three factual PR/progress highlights derived from comparable submitted source observations.

**SUPERSEDED CURRENT-DECISION INPUT (M4/M6):** The former exposure-confirmation policy consumed `Controlled`/`Breakdown`/`Not assessed` answers. Current recommendation decisions do not consume those values, current workouts do not write them, and the completion UI does not display them. Historical values remain immutable compatibility bytes only.

A catalog-recognized added exercise immediately receives the canonical prescription. An uncatalogued exercise receives a stable `user_custom_*` performance identity and remains fully loggable, but no recommendation is generated until its profile confirms primary canonical muscle, resistance mode, exercise style, progression metric, and—for load/assistance progression—the smallest increment. Secondary muscle is optional. A complete profile can receive bounded sets/reps/RPE/RIR/rest/progression guidance from muscle-level research, goal policy, and exact same-identity history. Its explanation explicitly withholds canonical exercise ranking, biomechanics, substitution, and equivalence claims; no automatic research crosswalk is created. Completing or editing the profile rebuilds unfinished work only.

Load history has two distinct anchors. The latest valid normal exposure is the ordinary next-session baseline; the historical high watermark is the strongest valid top performance (estimated performance then load/reps for external resistance, or least assistance with rep/RPE tie-breaking for assisted bodyweight). Planned reductions, pain, and explicitly invalid technique are excluded. A qualified normal progression moves only one available equipment increment from the latest load; when below the watermark, that step is capped at the watermark rather than jumping through it. After more than the implemented 56-day return gap, the first baseline chooses the lower-stress of the latest comparable load and approximately 90% of the valid external-load watermark rounded down to available equipment; assisted work chooses the easier of the latest assistance and approximately 10% more assistance rounded conservatively. The watermark is displayed in the rationale but is never an automatic command to reproduce an old maximum. Deload and temporary readiness reductions still supersede this anchor. These are **IMPLEMENTED HEURISTICS**, not universal training laws (`prescribedLoadFromHistory`, `setPrescriptionForRole`, engine/set-prescription tests).

A top set must meet its own programmed rep maximum and RPE ceiling before its candidate next load is actionable. A back-off structure must have every programmed comparable back-off set at or above its role-specific rep maximum and at or below its RPE ceiling; until then, the UI labels the progression state conditional/hold-load. This preserves gradual progression while keeping the recommendation summary, role evidence, and editable rows on one executable session plan (`app-foundation.js:coachTargetForTemplateExercise`, `app-analysis.js:setPrescriptionForRole`, `app-views.js:renderRolePrescriptionDetails`, `scripts/test-prescription-app-integration.js`, `scripts/test-set-prescriptions.js`).

Prior-workout evidence uses one resistance-aware resolver for every exercise. “Last time” searches every submitted workout through the requested date, including records older than the six-month trend-analysis window. Stable performance identity remains primary; exact normalized Strong name is the fallback when imported identity provenance drifted, so differently named equipment/attachment variations stay separate. Comparable-set selection prefers the same normalized role and role index; when Strong import classification and the current programmed structure differ, it falls back to the same ordered working-set position from the newest eligible workout rather than discarding the exposure. Warm-ups, skipped/unknown work, the current session, and inappropriate deload baselines remain excluded. Missing RPE reduces evidence completeness but does not make a completed load/rep set disappear.

A prescription snapshot with zero working sets or no usable rep range is invalid for live workout construction. The saved template/imported-history set count and roles remain the fallback authority, guaranteeing at least one working set without fabricating performance history. This validity guard is separate from confidence: sparse evidence can still yield a usable conservative template target, while genuinely absent history is labeled explicitly.

An unmapped Strong-only exercise with exact submitted history uses its newest eligible load/repetition sets and the saved template roles under `recommendationStatus: history_fallback`. Missing Strong RPE stays missing as evidence; it does not erase the completed load/repetition record. Research progression is explicitly unavailable until a real crosswalk exists. Collision, corrupted-snapshot, engine-failure, illness, and applicable pain paths do not use this fallback. Localized pain still blocks an unresolved-taxonomy exercise because the affected muscle cannot be verified safely.

Readiness/fatigue adjustment changes the smallest sufficient lever. A moderate signal normally preserves load and reps, removes one set only when at least three are programmed, and lowers target RPE by 0.5; a severe multi-domain signal may reduce one set, load by about 5%, and RPE by 1. A planned reduced/light prescription is stored with the performed exposure. Analytics exclude that exposure from weakness/regression baselines and label it as a planned reduction; prescribed lower work must not generate a “performance declined” fatigue flag. Reassessment uses subsequent comparable normal exposures.

Set structures are straight sets, top-set/back-off, multiple top sets, single working set, or custom. A compound may use top/back-off; isolation work uses straight sets when a peak set adds no value. When `finalPrescription` supplies explicit role targets, workout construction and recommendation explanation preserve that structure instead of applying legacy light-session role collapsing. Conflicting personal structure summaries are surfaced rather than silently chosen (`chooseSetStructure`, corresponding test).

The engine’s default policy includes minimum comparable exposures 3, moderate 5, high 8, plateau window 3, regression window 2, minimum rep-range width 2, back-off reduction 8–18% (12% target), maximum rep loss 25%, and maximum back-off reduction 30%. These are **IMPLEMENTED HEURISTICS**, not claimed universal science (`DEFAULT_POLICY`).

Volume does not automatically increase on a plateau and reduces under regression (`determineVolumePrescription`). Mesocycle specialization and lower-fatigue factors are implemented policy values (1.2 and 0.72), while default deload volume factor is 0.5. Any change requires engine tests and contract review.

## Fatigue, staleness, rotation, and deload

`assessExerciseStaleness` evaluates recent comparable exposures, progression/e1RM trends, RPE slope, rep loss, back-off drop, recovery cost, pain, adherence, and alternative advantage. Time alone cannot rotate a still-progressing lift. Classifications include productive, approaching plateau, stalled, regressing, excessively fatiguing, rotation candidate, and insufficient evidence.

Implemented notable thresholds include recovery cost at least 60 on repeated exposures, RPE slope at least 0.3, two recent regressions (including less than -1.5% comparisons), and score-based classifications. These are deterministic operational rules in `prescription-engine.js`, not externally validated universal thresholds.

`assessDeloadNeed` scopes action:

- One affected exercise → exercise-specific deload.
- Multiple degraded exercises for a muscle → muscle-group deload.
- Widespread decline plus persistent systemic suppression → full-program deload.
- One poor HRV value → never sufficient for full-program deload.

Pain blocks automatic progression and can support substitution/rotation; medical diagnosis is outside scope. Engine `3.3.8` applies this precedence to normalized exercise history as well as the current readiness check-in: pain on the latest comparable exposure or repeated pain in the staleness window enters the canonical hard-safety path before any exercise-deload decision. The painful original is non-executable, has zero working sets and no prescribed load, and requires a confirmed pain-free substitute or stopping the affected work. The date-normalized history rule is input-order invariant. Regression without pain can still produce a bounded executable exercise deload.

**SUPERSEDED APPLICATION PATH (M6):** The former safety-locked substitute UI and global exclusion revalidation path are removed. The sole current resolver may advise rest, modification, or substitution for directly recorded pain/illness, but it cannot block the logger. Ordinary substitution accepts a chosen exercise/variant without equipment or exclusion gating, preserves the completed workout as history, and keeps the source routine unchanged unless the user explicitly saves the change.

## Full-program mesocycle planning and candidate scoring — SUPERSEDED HISTORICAL RECORD (M3)

This is the authoritative program-planning section. The planner uses a two-stage process: first choose one program-wide exercise portfolio, then distribute that portfolio across sessions. A `Program Slot` is a muscle-group coverage and exercise-role requirement inside the complete plan; it replaces the former ambiguous “Prescription Block” term. Each slot states its required selection count, weekly sets/exposures, selected exercise, candidate alternatives, planned sessions, and rationale. Five candidates means up to five alternatives for a slot, never five mandatory weekly exercises.

Candidate discovery evaluates the entire compatible research exercise library plus traceable personal/current sources. `Current Program Exercises` means exercises in templates attached to the active mesocycle. `Recent Exercises` means submitted exercise exposures within the last 56 days. `Previously Successful Exercises` requires adequate comparable personal history. `Eligible Exercise Library` is the compatible canonical research library after equipment and restriction filters. Every displayed candidate carries a source trace; equipment exclusions are retained with an inspectable reason. Aliases must normalize to one canonical research ID. Camber Bar Bench Press, Cambered Bench Press, and Cambered Barbell Bench Press resolve to `ex_cambered_barbell_bench_press`.

**SUPERSEDED HISTORICAL RECORD (M3):** The former planner equipment alternatives and their schemas/tests were deleted with the planner. Legacy backup decoding is isolated and does not expose this compatibility capability to runtime callers.

`Predicted Program Effectiveness` estimates usefulness for the proposed slot and plan; it does not guarantee hypertrophy. The implemented score is 72% isolated target-muscle recommendation strength and 28% full-program fit. The isolated score already blends personal hypertrophy/progression/recovery/repeatability/tolerance evidence with muscle specificity, lengthened loading, stability, progression ease, fatigue cost, research support, and personal confidence. Full-program fit then adjusts for redundant patterns, local/systemic fatigue, spinal load, grip demand, joint stress, equipment, current portfolio, and lower-fatigue role value. Candidate ordering is recomputed after selection. Confidence separately describes evidence quality; Evidence identifies the sources. Positive and limiting factors explain the score.

Mechanical redundancy requires a known shared primary movement pattern, overlapping primary muscle family, and compatible joint action. The implemented similarity also considers role, loading profile, stability, regional emphasis, and equipment; the review requires at least 0.82 similarity before showing a user-facing duplication warning. Unknown or missing patterns score zero and remain internal data-quality work rather than normal user review. Shared equipment, generic compound status, broad push/pull labels, or incidental secondary overlap cannot create redundancy. Repeated same-role hinges for one target can trigger review, while press/fly overlap remains complementary unless the actual role/loading profile is nearly identical. Systemic, spinal, grip, or joint conflicts remain separate fatigue/recovery findings.

Generation order is objective/schedule/equipment/scope; evidence-adjusted effective-volume targets; muscle priority; major compounds/high-value lifts; session distribution and recovery; secondary/isolation work; hard daily-limit enforcement; taxonomy-weighted volume calculation; automatic balancing; then unresolved actionable review. Major program muscles are allocated before smaller supplemental muscles. Research taxonomy 2.1.0 retains 23 canonical anatomical muscle IDs across 62 exercises and 151 relationships, then projects them into 20 programming families. Direct dynamic work receives 1.0, meaningful fractional work 0.5 or 0.25, and incidental/unknown/isometric relationships receive zero hypertrophy credit. The shared family ledger counts the strongest qualifying relationship once per exercise/family, keeps local fatigue additive, and rounds only final exposed totals. Credited fractional work contributes to the same guided effective-set target as direct work; the UI preserves the breakdown and separately warns when frequency, distribution, or evidence confidence remains inadequate. Prescription history exposes the same family projection and versions while retaining legacy canonical totals for compatible readers.

The retained automatic generator may schedule each canonical exercise on only one day. Guided plans may intentionally reuse the same exercise across different days. Within one training day, the same canonical exercise or alias is invalid: candidate selection disables it, domain validation rejects it, move/import paths surface it, and viability blocks legacy duplicates. The intended correction is to merge or restructure the existing assignment, not add a duplicate card.

Muscle scope is user-controlled. `includedMuscleGroupIds` filters candidate pools and program slots before portfolio construction; the engine never silently re-adds an excluded group. The snapshot retains all available groups, included groups, and structured omissions. Omitted major groups receive a fuller balance/movement-coverage explanation; smaller groups receive a concise optional-goal explanation. Any omission requires explicit `scopeConfirmed` acknowledgement before the draft can become planned or active. A confirmed omission is intentional scope, not a volume-validation failure.

Session construction uses named split anchors by training-day count (full body, upper/lower, push/pull, and accessories as applicable), then applies primary/supporting role fit, local overlap, adjacent-session recovery, high-fatigue movement spacing, systemic fatigue, spinal load, grip demand, joint stress, duration, and equipment constraints. Distinct selected exercises that serve the same program slot receive a strong placement cost when that slot is already represented in a candidate session, so required weekly exposure frequency is distributed across compatible days instead of being concentrated accidentally. Repeated high-fatigue exercises on consecutive training days are blocking. Review also blocks materially inadequate effective sets or frequency. Above-range volume, redundancy, grip concentration, and similar correctable conditions are warnings unless another rule elevates them. Engine transition logic refuses activation while a blocking issue remains; this is not only a UI restriction.

Implemented policy defaults remain 75-minute target/100-minute maximum sessions, no more than three high-fatigue compounds per session, maximum session spinal load 180, and grip demand 190. A practical session normally has five to eight exercises. Every constructed session has a hard maximum of 18 working sets and two exercises targeting one muscle; warm-ups are excluded. Placement rejects allocations that would exceed either hard cap. If the requested scope cannot fit, lower-priority work remains unallocated and a blocking schedule-capacity finding offers more days, less scope/direct volume, maintenance volume, or a different objective.

Mesocycle lifecycle states are `draft`, `planned`, `active`, `completed`, `reviewed`, `abandoned`, and `archived`. Draft/planned/abandoned plans that were never activated may be deleted with confirmation. Completed plans are protected and may be archived/reviewed, not destructively deleted. Productive, tolerated exercises remain across objective changes; elapsed time alone never forces rotation. Readiness can adjust a started workout but never silently rewrites this base mesocycle.

## Research translation and guardrails

Research selection, population, grading, conflicts, and update policy live in `research_database/METHODOLOGY.md`; sources are indexed in `research_database/BIBLIOGRAPHY.md`. Research database 3.0.0 validates PubMed/PMC provenance where verified, rule-to-conclusion-to-study overlap, population exclusions, controlled vocabularies, and referential integrity. `rule_authority` distinguishes evidence, product policy, and safety; `enforcement_level` distinguishes advisory rules from a narrow allowlist of deterministic hard blockers. Unsupported numeric or timing policies remain disclosed, configurable product judgments rather than settled science. Importers must preserve authority, enforcement, and policy disclosure fields.

Guardrails:

- Null/unknown is not zero.
- Sparse or non-comparable evidence fails toward hold, research default, or insufficient data.
- Pain/invalid technique blocks automatic progression.
- One readiness marker cannot cause a systemic deload.
- Nutrition is not double-counted as multiple independent signals.
- Readiness changes today, not the base plan.
- Manual overrides are append-only, explained, locked for the workout, and evaluable afterward.
- Historical snapshots retain engine/schema/personal/research versions and are not silently recomputed.
