# 01 · Control Chart — `coach-concepts/01-control-chart.html`

**Thesis:** Every lift lives inside its own normal. The coach's job is to know your normal and tell
you the instant you leave it.

**Organizing primitive:** a statistical process control chart (Shewhart style). For each lift the
coach draws your expected value for *this set* (centre line, recovery-adjusted), with ±1σ and ±2σ
bands as tinted zones and hairline control limits. Every logged set drops a point. The Strong-fast
logger lives directly beneath the chart — logging a set is literally plotting it.

**Frames:**
- F1 — Bench chart with set 1 plotted at 9 (inside band, success ink), set 2's expected window
  (8–9) waiting as an open target zone. Under it, the logger rows (set, previous, lb, reps, ✓).
  A second, slim chart for **rest interval** rides along (rest is also a controlled variable).
- F2 — Set 2 lands at 6: the point breaches the lower control limit, ringed in amber, with a rule
  label in control-chart language ("Below lower limit · drop of 3 is 3.4σ beyond your usual
  0.6 ± 0.7"). The rest chart beneath shows the cause visually: 1:32 plotted far below the 2:30
  band. Recovery shown as the shifted centre line ("centre moved −0.4 reps for HRV −27%"). e1RM
  mini-chart proves strength is stable (253 vs 249–254 band).
- F3 — Options drawn as **projected hollow points**: 165 × 8 lands dead-centre in a re-drawn band;
  185 + 3:00 rest lands at the lower limit; stopping leaves an empty slot. Downstream: incline
  3 → 2 sets, weekly chest 15/16 inside a 12–18 band.
- F4 — Plan applied: band re-centred for 165, set 3 target zone active, rest chart showing the
  timer holding to 2:30.
- F5 — Thursday: next-session chart with the hold at 185, the progression threshold as a labelled
  upper line ("≥ 9 on sets 1–2 → 190"), the watch rule as a run-rule ("2nd breach with normal
  recovery → pressing deload"), and the HRV < 45 contingency.

**Feel:** instrument-grade precision — hairlines, fine grids, tabular numerals, small caps labels,
lots of white. Calm until something leaves the band, then unmistakable.

**Type:** IBM Plex Sans + IBM Plex Mono.

**Must not become:** a generic progress dashboard of line charts, or certainty rendered as
solid/dashed weighting. The band-and-limit grammar is the whole idea.
